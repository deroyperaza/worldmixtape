What this is

World Mixtape is an experiment in exploring the world through its music.

Navigate a world map, choose any country, and travel through its decades — from the early 1900s to right now. Every track is locally produced popular music: made by people from that place (natives and diaspora), not imports. It's a way to hear how a country actually sounds, decade by decade.

And because music is a doorway, not a destination, each country is wrapped in context — photos, an outline map, key facts, its most important people, and an honest, plain-spoken history timeline. A song becomes a way in to a place.

The curation lens

What makes this specific is how the music is chosen. For each country and each decade, the collection blends two things:

  • Culturally significant local music — the songs that mattered where they were made.
  • Hand-picked artists from my own world-music library, because I've spent years falling in love with music from everywhere.

This isn't about digging up rare gems — that's a different, beautiful game. It's about familiarizing yourself with local popular culture: the music people there grew up on and made their own.

Why it exists

Popular music is created everywhere, and all of it deserves to be explored. This is for the curious — for anyone who wants to get a feel for places they may never go, to explore local culture and creative output, and maybe crack open a door into a country's history.

It's built to reward curiosity, and to center a simple idea: every culture is valuable and interesting.

What inspired it

It grows out of two projects we made at Hyperakt — The Refugee Project and On The Grid — both worldwide explorations built to help people understand context, history, and perspective. Both were made before the era of AI.

The interface owes a debt to Radiooooo (yes, five o's) — a wonderful map-based, decade-organized music library curated by DJs. World Mixtape borrows its structure but changes the lens and the goal: where Radiooooo is about discovering gems, this is about familiarizing yourself with local popular culture, layered with the story of each place so it doubles as a learning experience.

Who built it — and how

Built by one person in five days, with AI.

World Mixtape was made at Hyperakt by Deroy Peraza, written in about five days using Claude Code. It's an experiment in how AI can help a single person aggregate and make sense of publicly available knowledge — and build an archive at a scale that used to take a team months to build.

The point isn't the tech. It's what the tech makes possible: using AI to further education and curiosity, to give voice to places that rarely get one, and to help democratize information.

Everything here is drawn from openly available sources — Wikipedia, YouTube, Wikimedia Commons, and other public data. World Mixtape is a non-commercial passion project; all music, images, and facts belong to their creators and sources.
